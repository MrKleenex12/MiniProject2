# Reflection: opticspy_lightpipes

LightPipes is a Python toolbox for simulating light propagation, mainly written by Fred van Goor. Its timeline is irregular with 9 gaps of at least three months, more than any other project in my set. That is what a one-developer project usually looks like: short bursts of work separated by long breaks.

The longest gap was 12 months, from January to December 2016, and it was easy to interpret. The commits before it are a documentation and release push, such as "edit examples," "add newest version," and "edit README." Once that was finished, there was nothing left to do, and no issue tracker existed yet to bring in requests.

The recovery came with a new partner. In January 2017, Fred added faster FFTW3 builds, and at the same time a new contributor, guyskk, opened issues like "Init repository," "Invite more members," and "build linux wheels." This means the project came back because it moved into the opticspy organization with someone handling packaging. The project is Active, but only barely, with its last commit in February 2026 after a year and a half of quiet.
